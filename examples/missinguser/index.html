Why CAPSULE?

First entry

Because I got tired of the answer being "trust us".

Every service I used to send a file encrypted it in transit and at rest, and every one of them kept the ability to read it. That ability is the part that gets subpoenaed, breached, scanned and sold. The interesting question was never whether something is encrypted. It is who holds the key.

Publishing has the same shape. A domain is rented, a certificate is issued, a host serves the bytes. Three parties, any of whom can be leaned on to make a page disappear or say something else, and all three send an invoice. I wanted an address nobody sold me and nobody can take back.

So the rule underneath all of this is a small one: a server should be able to hold your data without being able to read it, and the thing that unlocks it should never travel the same road. Files are encrypted before they leave. The key rides in the part of a link that browsers never send to a server. A relay holds bytes it cannot open, for a time it did not choose, with no account attached.

I care about this because information wants to move and the current arrangement charges rent at every step. Not only in money. In permission. Somebody decides whether your page stays up, whether your file is allowed, whether your name is still yours next year. Removing that somebody is not a feature. It is the whole point.

What is honest to say today: the network is small, so the protection is small. A mix path across relays one person operates protects nobody, and right now that is close to what exists. The design does not cap how good it gets as people join, and nothing else about it matters until they do.

That is why this page is here rather than in a README. It is the thing working, and you are reading it through the network it describes.
